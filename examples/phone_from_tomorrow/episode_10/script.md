# Episode 10: Delete My Number

Dawn filled the exchange, and Tobi was still holding the choice in his hand.

Delete my number and lose her forever. Keep it and save her once.

For the first time, Mara stepped in front of the phone instead of behind it.

Do not save me, she said. I will not be the next first owner.

Then Iya Sade finally told the ending the old stories always hid.

The chain only breaks when an owner refuses the trade and lets the phone go dark.

The bus woman stepped from the rows and said she had been the very first owner, long erased.

Every dead phone rang at once with the voices of the lives he had already saved.

The messages had never been from tomorrow. They were the saved, begging not to be erased.

Keeping the phone meant feeding it Mara to free the rest.

Mara closed her hand over his on the phone.

Instead of choosing, Tobi dialed his own number one last time.

He told his future self that no one else would pay for him again.

For the first time, the voice on the other end had nothing left to say.

He brought up the number and held his thumb over delete.

He chose the ending the phone could not survive, and pressed it.

Every receiver in the exchange went silent together.

The first owner finally let go, fading into the daylight she was owed.

And when the light filled the room, Mara was still standing there.

They walked out into Lagos morning, the phone finally dead in his pocket.

Behind them, on a market stall, one old phone flickered faintly green.
