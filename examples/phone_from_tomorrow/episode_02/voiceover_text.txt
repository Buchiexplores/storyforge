The package outside Tobi's door was breathing.

He did not open it. He dragged Mara away from the hallway and waited until sunrise.

At 6:12 AM, the cracked phone lit up again.

This time, it showed a bus plate: KJA-404. Do not let it reach Third Mainland.

Tobi had a delivery due in twenty minutes. Missing it meant losing the only job still answering his calls.

Mara told him to call the police. Tobi looked at the phone and saw one more line.

The driver will not be inside.

By 6:45, Lagos was already loud. Horns, rainwater, generator smoke, everything moving except Tobi.

Then he saw it: a yellow danfo with the exact plate number, rolling through traffic like somebody invisible had both hands on the wheel.

The driver's seat was empty.

People laughed when Tobi shouted. In Lagos, every bus looks haunted when you are late for work.

So he jumped in front of it.

The bus stopped one inch from his knees.

Inside, every passenger was asleep with their phones glowing green.

One woman opened her eyes and whispered, Tobi Adeyemi, you already changed tomorrow once.

Before he could move, his phone buzzed again.

New message: Check the back seat.

There, on the torn leather, was the same brown package from his doorway.
